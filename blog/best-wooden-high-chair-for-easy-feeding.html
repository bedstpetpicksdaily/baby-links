Frequently Asked Questions

What features make a wooden high chair easier for feeding?

Look for high chairs with a wide, flat tray that's easy to clean and doesn't have deep crevices where food gets stuck. A removable tray is essential so you can quickly wipe it down or wash it in the sink between meals. Additionally, chairs with a footrest help babies sit upright and stable, which improves their ability to self-feed and reduces mess.

Are wooden high chairs easier to clean than plastic ones?

Wooden high chairs can be just as easy to clean if they have smooth finishes and removable trays, but they require more careful maintenance to avoid water damage and staining. Sealed or varnished wood resists stains better than unfinished wood, and you should wipe spills immediately rather than letting liquids soak in. Plastic trays are generally more stain-resistant, but quality wooden chairs with removable trays clean up just as quickly.

What size tray should a high chair have for feeding?

A tray should be at least 20-24 inches wide to provide enough space for a plate, cup, and utensils without items falling off easily. The depth should be at least 12-14 inches to catch spills and prevent food from sliding onto your baby's lap. Make sure the tray has slightly raised edges or a subtle lip to contain messes while still allowing easy access for your child to grab food.

How do I know if a wooden high chair is safe and stable for feeding?

Check that the chair has a wide, sturdy base that won't tip when your baby leans forward or side to side during meals. Look for a five-point harness system to keep your child securely seated, and verify that all wood joints are tight with no splinters or loose parts. Read customer reviews specifically about stability and durability, and ensure the chair meets CPSC safety standards for high chairs.
