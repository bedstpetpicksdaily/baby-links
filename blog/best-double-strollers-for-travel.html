Best Double Strollers for Travel

Traveling with two little ones is a completely different animal than a trip to the neighborhood park — and the double stroller that works beautifully on your suburban sidewalk can become a genuine liability at gate check, in a hotel elevator, or wedged into the trunk of a European rental car. Airlines are increasingly strict about gate-check dimensions, cobblestone streets and airport curbs punish flimsy wheels, and every extra pound matters when you're also carrying a diaper bag, a car seat, and a toddler who has decided walking is no longer a service she offers. The right travel double stroller isn't necessarily the plushest or the most feature-packed — it's the one you can fold with one hand while holding a boarding pass in your teeth.

What to Look For

  • A genuinely compact fold — and a realistic weight. Look for a folded footprint under roughly 32" x 20" x 16" and a weight under 30 pounds. Side-by-side doubles often fold flatter but wider; tandems fold narrower but taller. Measure your actual car trunk and, if you're flying, check whether your airline allows the stroller at the gate or requires it to be checked at the counter (most allow gate check for strollers, but oversized doubles are frequently bumped to the counter).
  • Self-standing fold and a carry strap or handle. This sounds minor until you're standing on a jet bridge with no free hands. A stroller that stands upright when folded can lean against a wall while you wrangle kids, and a padded shoulder strap turns an awkward 25-pound object into something you can actually walk down an aisle with.
  • Wheels sized for the terrain you're actually visiting. Small hard plastic wheels are fine for airport terminals and resort walkways, but they're miserable on cobblestones, sand, gravel paths, or the uneven sidewalks of older cities. If your trip involves anything rougher than smooth pavement, prioritize larger rubber or foam-filled wheels with real suspension — even if it costs you a bit of compactness.

Our Top Pick

The Bugaboo Donkey 5 Twin is our overall recommendation for families who want one stroller that handles both the airport and the actual destination. Its standout feature for travel is the side-by-side-to-single conversion: it narrows to 23.6" in mono mode to squeeze through hotel doorways, shop aisles, and airport security lanes, then widens to full twin mode when you need both seats. The fold is one-piece and self-standing, and the underseat basket is enormous — big enough to swallow a packed diaper bag plus a folded travel blanket, which meaningfully reduces what you have to carry.

For families prioritizing pure packability over plushness, the Zoe Twin+ (XL2) is the more budget- and airline-friendly answer. At around 17 pounds with a one-hand fold, it's the lightest legitimate side-by-side double on the market, it fits through standard 30" doorways, and it comes with a carry strap and travel bag included. Both seats recline fully flat, and it's rated from birth with the appropriate infant insert. If you're doing multiple flights, subway systems, or lots of stairs, the Zoe wins on sheer maneuverability.

Our third pick, particularly for theme park trips and long airport days, is the Baby Jogger City Mini GT2 Double. The quick-fold mechanism is the fastest in the category — grab the strap, pull, done — and the all-terrain forever-air tires and adjustable handlebar make it far more comfortable for tall parents and rough surfaces than most compact doubles.

Why This Works for This Situation

Travel exposes weaknesses that never show up at home. At home, a heavy stroller lives in the garage and gets rolled ten feet to the car. On a trip, you may lift it into an overhead-free luggage compartment, carry it up a flight of Metro stairs, fold it in a taxi line while it's raining, and unfold it a dozen times in a single day. Every one of the picks above is chosen because the fold is fast and repeatable under stress — not because it has the nicest fabric. The Donkey's narrow mode solves the single most common travel complaint about side-by-sides (they don't fit through doors), while the Zoe solves the second most common one (they're too heavy to carry).

There's also a durability argument. Gate-checked strollers get thrown, dropped, and stacked under suitcases, and cheap plastic joints and unsupported frames are the first things to crack. The Bugaboo and Baby Jogger both use aluminum frames with metal-reinforced stress points and have widely available replacement parts, which matters when you're three days into a two-week trip. Whatever you choose, invest $30–$50 in a padded travel bag — it dramatically reduces gate-check damage and gives you a place to stuff the rain cover, sunshade, and the inevitable snacks.

What to Avoid

  • Full-size side-by-side joggers. They're glorious on trails and awful everywhere else on a trip — typically 33"+ wide, 35+ pounds, and too large for most gate-check policies. If you need a jogger, keep it at home and travel with a second, lighter stroller.
  • Umbrella-style doubles with tiny hard wheels and no recline. They're cheap and light, but they're punishing on anything but perfect pavement, they tip backward when you hang a bag on the handles, and they can't accommodate a napping infant. Naps on the go are the entire point of a travel stroller.
  • Anything with a two-piece or multi-step fold. If the stroller requires removing seats, detaching a bassinet, or unlatching in a specific sequence before it collapses, you will grow to resent it by day two. Test the fold in the store while holding something in one hand — that's the real-world test.
  • Buying a travel system you can't gate check. Some inline doubles exceed airline size limits when the second seat is attached. Call the airline or check the stroller's folded dimensions against their published policy before you fly.

The best travel double stroller is the one that makes you say "let's just go" instead of "is it worth the hassle" — and that usually comes down to fold speed, weight, and width, in that order. Measure your trunk, check your airline's gate-check limits, and take a look at our full double stroller comparison chart to find the model that matches the trip you're actually taking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the lightest double stroller for travel?

The lightest double strollers typically weigh between 15-20 pounds, with models like the Zoe XL2 and Baby Trend Sit N Stand weighing around 17-19 pounds. Look for lightweight aluminum frames and compact designs that fold small enough to fit in airplane overhead bins or car trunks. Weight combined with compact folding is more important than weight alone when traveling.

Can you take a double stroller on an airplane?

Yes, most airlines allow you to check one double stroller for free or gate-check it at boarding. Check your specific airline's policy beforehand, as some have size restrictions—generally strollers must fold to fit through standard baggage doors and weigh under 50 pounds. Gate-checking (bringing it to the plane door) is often the safest option to avoid damage.

What's the best double stroller for narrow spaces and travel?

Tandem (front-to-back) strollers like the Baby Jogger City Mini GT2 Double are ideal for tight spaces since they take up less width than side-by-side models, making them easier to navigate airports, restaurants, and hotel hallways. Side-by-side strollers are wider but generally lighter and easier to fold, so choose based on whether you prioritize maneuverability or ease of setup during travel.

Are expensive double strollers worth it for travel?

Mid-range strollers ($300-600) often offer the best value for travel, with quality wheels, smooth folding mechanisms, and reasonable durability without premium brand markups. Expensive models ($700+) may add luxury features but not necessarily better travel performance; focus instead on lightweight, compact design, sturdy build quality, and good customer reviews specific to traveling parents.
