Our Verdict

The Doona Liki Trike S3+ deserves its 4.3-star rating because it reliably does what it promises, but 'reliably does what it promises' doesn't automatically equal 'worth the premium.' If you value convertibility, don't want multiple pieces of gear cluttering your life, and have the budget, this is a safe purchase—you'll use it for 2-3 years without mechanical failure. However, budget-conscious parents shouldn't feel pressured. Spending $150 on a solid traditional tricycle plus $100 on a decent compact stroller gives you nearly identical functionality for less money, even if it's less elegant. This trike shines for second or third children when space and simplicity matter most, or for parents who genuinely value the seamless transitions between modes. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Doona Liki Trike S3+ actually worth $300+?

That depends entirely on your priorities. If you're in an apartment, frequently travel, or have limited storage, the space savings and single-purchase convenience justify the premium. If you have a garage and aren't bothered by owning separate items, you're throwing away money here. Budget alternatives like the Qplay Rito or Besrey trikes offer 80% of the functionality for 50% of the cost, though they lack the elegant stroller integration.

At what age does my kid actually use this independently?

Parent-controlled mode works from 10 months. By 18-24 months, most toddlers can pedal independently, though they'll still need steering assistance to avoid lampposts and other pedestrians. The full independence mode (hands-off steering) is genuinely useful around 2.5-3 years, so you're getting 2-3 years of solid use depending on when you start.

How does it compare to the regular Doona Liki Trike (not S3+)?

The S3+ adds a slightly upgraded frame, improved wheel suspension, and a smoother conversion mechanism compared to earlier versions. The original Liki Trike is still solid, but if you're buying new in September 2026, the S3+ is the current model. The price difference between S3+ and used original models is usually $50-80, making the newer version worth choosing if availability is equal.

Does this work on uneven surfaces like gravel or grass?

Moderately well. The wheels are decent enough that mulch paths and mowed grass don't cause problems, but true gravel roads will shake your kid around more than you'd like. It's designed for paved surfaces primarily. If most of your outings happen on hiking trails or unpaved parks, a traditional all-terrain tricycle handles those better than any compact convertible model.
