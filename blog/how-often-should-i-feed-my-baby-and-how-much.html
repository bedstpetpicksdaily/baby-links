How Often Should I Feed My Baby and How Much?

Newborns should be fed every 2-3 hours (8-12 times daily), while babies 6 months and older typically eat 3-4 times daily with solid foods introduced. The exact amount depends on your baby's age, weight, and whether you're breastfeeding or formula feeding.

The Short Answer

Feeding frequency and amounts change dramatically as your baby grows. Newborns require frequent feedings around the clock, but by 6 months, most babies can go longer between feedings. Always follow your pediatrician's guidance, as every baby is unique. Watch for hunger cues like rooting, hand-to-mouth movements, and fussiness rather than relying solely on schedules.

The Full Explanation

Newborns (0-3 months): Feed every 2-3 hours, totaling 8-12 feedings per day. If breastfeeding, aim for 10-15 minutes per breast. If formula feeding, offer 2-3 ounces per feeding initially, increasing to 4-5 ounces by 3 months. Your baby's stomach is tiny, so frequent small meals are necessary.

4-6 months: Feeding intervals extend to 4-5 hours, with 6-8 feedings daily. Breastfed babies may take 15-20 minutes per breast. Formula-fed babies typically consume 5-6 ounces per feeding. Some babies may show readiness for solids around 6 months—consult your pediatrician before starting.

6-9 months: Introduce solid foods while continuing milk feedings 4-5 times daily. Offer 1-2 tablespoons of single-ingredient purees initially, gradually increasing to 2-3 tablespoons. Breast milk or formula remains the primary nutrition source alongside solids.

9-12 months: Progress to 3 milk feedings plus 2-3 solid food meals. Offer 3-4 tablespoons of purees or soft finger foods at each meal. Babies may self-regulate intake, so watch for fullness cues like turning away or slowing down.

12+ months: Transition to 3 meals plus 2 snacks with milk as a beverage alongside food. Dairy intake should remain around 16-24 ounces daily from milk, yogurt, or cheese. Most toddlers can eat regular family foods with appropriate modifications for safety.

What the Experts Say

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends feeding on demand, responding to your baby's hunger and fullness cues rather than following rigid schedules. Pediatricians emphasize that babies are excellent self-regulators—they'll eat what their bodies need when allowed to do so naturally. Dr. Ruth Lawrence, a leading pediatric nutrition expert, notes that responsive feeding supports healthy growth and helps prevent both underfeeding and overfeeding. Always discuss feeding concerns with your pediatrician, especially if your baby seems uncomfortable, isn't gaining weight appropriately, or shows unusual feeding patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I feed my newborn baby?

Newborns should be fed every 2-3 hours, which typically means 8-12 times per day for the first few weeks. Feed on demand when your baby shows hunger cues like rooting, hand-to-mouth movements, or crying, rather than strictly by the clock. By 2 months, many babies naturally space out feedings to every 3-4 hours.

How much should I feed my baby per feeding?

Newborns typically consume 0.5-1 ounce per feeding in the first few days, increasing to 1-2 ounces by day 3-5, and reaching 2-3 ounces by 2 weeks of age. By 2 months, most babies take 4-5 ounces per feeding, and by 4 months, they may consume 4-6 ounces. If breastfeeding, let your baby nurse for 10-20 minutes per side as hunger and milk supply dictate.

When should I start feeding my baby solid food?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends introducing solid foods around 6 months of age when your baby shows signs of readiness like sitting up with minimal support and loss of the tongue-thrust reflex. Start with single-ingredient purees like iron-fortified baby cereal and gradually introduce other foods, beginning with just 1-2 teaspoons per feeding. Continue breastfeeding or formula feeding as the primary nutrition source until 12 months.

How do I know if my baby is eating enough?

Signs your baby is eating enough include 6-8 wet diapers and 3-4 bowel movements per day by week 1, steady weight gain, and contentment after feedings. Track feeding duration and the number of wet diapers during the first month, and have your pediatrician check weight gain at regular checkups. If your baby seems lethargic, has fewer wet diapers, or isn't gaining weight, contact your doctor immediately.
