How does the ISOFIX base compare to seatbelt installation for safety?

ISOFIX removes the single biggest installation variable—user error in seatbelt tension. The i-Size compliance here means side-impact protection is engineered into the seat structure itself. For most families, especially those in vehicles with modern ISOFIX anchorages (standard since 2011 in Europe, 2013 in US models), ISOFIX is objectively more reliable. The green indicator light on this model removes the guesswork. That said, seatbelt-only vehicles don't lose meaningful protection—the seat itself is the safety device, not the connection method. Test your vehicle's ISOFIX accessibility before assuming it's the right choice for you.

Can you really use this from newborn through toddler years without buying another seat?

Yes, with a transition caveat. The I-Level I-Size handles newborns to approximately 105cm height (roughly 4 years old) in rear-facing mode. The removable newborn insert is essential for tiny babies—it fills the gap and positions their head correctly. Once your child hits 75cm or so (usually 18-24 months), the insert comes out, and they sit in the deeper cup. The headrest grows through sixteen positions, so you're genuinely adjusting to their anatomy as they develop, not just leaving them in an oversized bucket. After 105cm, you'll need a high-backed booster, but that's standard for all I-Size seats.
