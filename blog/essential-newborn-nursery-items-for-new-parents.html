Setting up a nursery for your newborn can feel overwhelming with countless products claiming to be essential, but having the right items in place creates a safe, comfortable space where your baby can sleep peacefully and you can care for them with confidence. The first few months are crucial for establishing routines, and a well-prepared nursery removes stress during those sleep-deprived nights. Understanding which items truly matter—and which are marketing hype—will help you create a functional space without breaking the bank or cluttering the room with unnecessary gear.

What to Look For

  • Safety certifications and standards: Prioritize items that meet CPSC (Consumer Product Safety Commission) standards and have current safety certifications. Cribs, mattresses, and bumpers should all comply with current regulations, and you'll want to verify that secondhand items haven't been recalled.
  • Durability and longevity: Choose pieces that can grow with your child or transition to other uses as your baby develops. A quality changing table can become a dresser, and a convertible crib can adapt from newborn to toddler to full-size bed, giving you better value over time.
  • Ease of cleaning and maintenance: Newborns are messy, so select items with removable, washable covers and surfaces that wipe down easily. Waterproof mattress protectors, machine-washable crib sheets, and wipeable changing pad covers will save you tremendous time during those inevitable accidents.

  • Overly decorative or themed furniture: Resist the temptation to invest heavily in matching nursery themes with coordinating dressers, wall decals, and specialty furniture.     Frequently Asked Questions

    What are the absolute must-have items for a newborn nursery?

    The essentials include a safe crib or bassinet, firm mattress with fitted sheets, a dresser for clothes and diapers, blackout curtains to aid sleep, a reliable room thermometer to maintain 68-72°F, and adequate lighting including a dimmable nightlight. You'll also need a comfortable chair or glider for feeding and bonding, storage for supplies, and a white noise machine to help your baby sleep through household sounds.

    Do I need a changing table or can I use a dresser?

    A dedicated changing table with safety straps is ideal for ergonomics and safety, but a dresser with a changing pad on top works just as well if it's at a comfortable height (around 36 inches). The key is having a non-slip changing pad and keeping all diaper supplies within arm's reach so you never leave your baby unattended.

    How many crib sheets and blankets do I need for a newborn?

    You'll need 3-4 fitted crib sheets so you always have clean ones while others are being washed. Skip traditional blankets entirely—use a sleep sack or wearable blanket instead, as loose blankets increase SIDS risk and are unsafe for newborns under 12 months.

    What nursery safety items do I actually need?

    Install a baby monitor with video and sound, use a firm mattress that fits snugly in the crib with no gaps, and keep the room at the proper temperature with a reliable thermometer. Add outlet covers, secure any furniture to walls to prevent tipping, and remove any cords, blind strings, or soft objects that could pose a strangulation hazard.
